Before work begins

Check what you inherited before you build on it.

One home may come fully documented, while one bought recently may have wiring, outlets and fixtures that the previous owner changed without leaving drawings, invoices or notes. In either home, a tidy cover plate says nothing about the work behind it.

Before a new project depends on an existing circuit, spare panel space or a garage feed, gather the closing documents, the home inspection report and any labels left behind. Then ask for that existing item to be assessed first.

How do I find out what electrical work the previous owner did?

Start with the seller disclosure, the home inspection report and any invoices or ESA paperwork passed on at closing. Where records are missing, a Licensed Electrical Contractor can examine the visible installation and say what can and cannot be confirmed.

Can I rely on a spare outlet or circuit that came with the house?

Treat it as unverified until it has been assessed. Ask the contractor to confirm what the circuit serves, what condition it is in and whether it suits the equipment you intend to connect before you plan around it.

Are the circuit labels left by the previous owner still accurate?

They may be, but they could predate later changes. Photograph the panel door and its directory, note anything that seems wrong, such as a breaker labelled for a room that no longer exists, and ask the contractor to verify each label.

How should I compare quotes when nobody knows what is in the walls?

Ask each contractor to state what was examined, what remains unknown and how hidden conditions found during the work will be priced and approved. Quotes that separate known work from allowances for discoveries are easier to compare fairly.

Does sending the London enquiry form arrange a visit to my home?

Expect no visit from the form, only a review of your enquiry; this site is for reading and has no part in quotes or the work. Check any Licensed Electrical Contractor's company and licence, then agree any visit and scope, and lastly cost and dates.
