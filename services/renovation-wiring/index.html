Renovation wiring in London.

Finishing a basement in a London home is the point to decide which existing wiring along the joists can stay before framing begins. If you recently bought the house, some runs may be undocumented additions.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your London property

Gather the basement floor plan, the intended use of each room and photographs of exposed joists, cables and junction boxes. Note any outlet or light down there that does not seem to belong to a labelled circuit.

Ask which existing basement wiring can be kept, which must be replaced and how the new lighting and outlets will be laid out. Confirm when the ESA review happens relative to insulation and drywall.

What should the assessment establish?

A list of existing cable runs opens the scope for finishing a London basement, because unfinished space is where earlier changes are easy to make and easy to leave undocumented. Each entry shows whether the run is suitable to keep and what must change before it is concealed. Below that list come the lighting, receptacles and any dedicated circuits for the planned rooms. The building permit for the finishing work and the ESA notification for the electrical work are listed as separate matters. The scope ends by naming who handles each and when inspections fall in the schedule.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is it worth keeping the wiring the last owner ran in the basement?

Only if the contractor confirms it is suitable. Ask for each run to be identified and assessed, and for the scope to say plainly what stays, what is replaced and why, before anything disappears behind drywall.

When does the electrical work get reviewed in a basement renovation?

The ESA notification is filed for the electrical work, and review normally has to happen before walls and ceilings are covered. Ask the contractor to fit that review into the framing and drywall schedule so nothing is closed too early.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
